# Maintain templates without rewriting history

Load this only when changing a reusable template, reviewing compatibility, or preparing an explicitly requested saved/native-template update. Ordinary document drafting does not require a template rollout process.

## Identify the artifact first

Keep the local Markdown asset, a saved/native template, a page created from it, and the page's current content/version distinct. A new skill release does not update an installed template or migrate existing pages. Discover actual connector capabilities before promising native reads, exports, or writes; use [publishing guidance](publishing.md).

Assess added/removed fields, renamed stable requirement IDs, status semantics, macros/variables, and disclosure changes. Preserve the earlier revision and rationale. A search with limited coverage cannot establish that no old instances exist.

## Small representative checks

- Fill one realistic case and one sparse/blocked case. Confirm the template remains useful without fabricated people, dates, metrics, approval, or observed results.
- Compare a previously filled document against the proposed migration. Preserve source anchors, requirement/acceptance IDs, actual decisions, and recorded test or deployment evidence.
- Verify that local links and the package's version/checksums agree. If native rendering, variables, or rich content matter, test them through the actual authorized surface and state any missing access.
- Review the affected audience and actual sharing restrictions. Hidden properties, filtered views, and executive/customer summaries are not permission controls. Notion relations/formulas or legacy wiki macros do not become Confluence capabilities by copying their text.

Structural validation and checkbox counts do not establish semantic quality, approval, or native-template compatibility.

## Rollout only the requested action

Offer the exact template diff, migration options, impact, known limits, and recovery path. Do not bulk rewrite existing pages, alter restrictions, change native status, or overwrite saved templates from a request to improve local authoring assets.

When a native change is authorized and resolved, preserve unrelated rich content and use fresh target versions. Read back the resulting template and one relevant authored instance when accessible; otherwise separate the verified local change from unresolved native behavior.

Explain the change to affected authors through the requested channel only. Deprecating a template does not authorize deletion, archival, or migration of existing documents. Follow supplied owners and review triggers instead of inventing a universal quarterly cadence. Keep local changelog history intact and retain relevant migration/decision evidence.
