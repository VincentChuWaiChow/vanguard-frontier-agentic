## Avoid near-duplicate documents

- A charter records the proposed mandate and authority/funding boundaries; a project plan sequences delivery. A PRD defines requirements and a TDD explains design. Link those documents rather than restating them.
- A status report is a dated snapshot; release notes explain an actual release. A roadmap is directional, not evidence of release or customer availability.
- OKRs define outcomes; project tasks are possible initiatives, not achieved key results.
- An experiment records a protocol and observations; a retrospective reflects on a completed period. Do not manufacture results to fill either.
- A customer-impact assessment evaluates a proposed change; an incident postmortem reconstructs an actual incident.
- A working agreement records team-level proposals and confirmed agreements; it cannot change organizational policy, employment terms, or access rights.

## Evidence and compatibility

The public gallery and its eight selected detail pages, plus the separately linked upstream project-charter pattern, were inspected on 2026-11-11. Names and broad use cases are source inspiration; the complete local templates, prompts, and safeguards are independently authored.
